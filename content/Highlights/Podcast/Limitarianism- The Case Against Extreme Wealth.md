# Note
 Limitarianism, the concept of limiting extreme wealth, is not a new idea. Ancient societies like the Hebrews and Greeks recognized the dangers of wealth concentration and implemented measures to prevent it. Ingrid Robbins, a Dutch and Belgian economist and philosopher, argues that limiting wealth in the 21st century is morally justifiable and essential for a healthy society. She proposes a wealth cap of 10 million euros per person, a figure she believes allows for significant wealth accumulation while preventing harmful concentration.

Robbins' research suggests that there is surprisingly broad public support for limiting extreme wealth. Her work highlights the negative consequences of wealth concentration, including unethical behavior, historical injustices, and a widening gap between the haves and have-nots. She argues that limiting wealth could actually benefit society as a whole by promoting a more equitable distribution of resources, fostering innovation, and creating a more just and sustainable world.

Robbins acknowledges the practical challenges of implementing a wealth cap, but she outlines several approaches, including progressive taxation, wealth taxes, and limits on inheritance. She emphasizes that these measures are not about punishing success but about creating a more level playing field for everyone. While her proposals face criticism for being idealistic, Robbins advocates for a gradual, incremental approach that involves open dialogue, careful planning, and a willingness to experiment and adapt.

The key takeaway is that the way we structure our economies and societies has a profound impact on everyone's lives. Our choices about wealth distribution, taxation, and social safety nets determine who thrives and who struggles, who has opportunities and who gets left behind. Limiting extreme wealth could potentially transform our world by improving education, healthcare, and environmental protection. It's about recognizing that the way things are isn't the way they have to be. We have the power to create a more just, sustainable, and equitable future.
