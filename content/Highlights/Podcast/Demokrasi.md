# Note
[[Democracy]] refers to the participation of the people in governance, either directly or through representatives. Even in [[Ancient Greece]], thinkers like [[Socrates]], [[Plato]], and [[Aristotle]] criticized and even condemned it. However, due to the failure of alternatives and its status as an unattainable utopia, it remains the most ideal option.

As [[Winston Churchill]] famously stated, democracy is the worst form of government—except for all the others that have been tried. The failure of concepts like collective intelligence and the wisdom of crowds in politics may be because these are also utopian ideas. In this episode, we explore the challenges and historical journey of the public’s pursuit of self-determination.

The word democracy comes from the Greek "demos" (people) and "kratos" (power). The term politics has Arabic roots; in the [[Ottoman Empire]], it referred to public squares where people who committed crimes against the sultan were executed. Today, it means the art of managing state affairs. The word law also comes from Arabic and is the plural form of "right."

[[Athenian democracy]] was a direct democracy, whereas [[Rome]] practiced a representative one. Ancient Greek philosophers argued that the problem was not with democracy itself but with the people practicing it. Socrates claimed that the majority lacked the knowledge and experience required for political decisions, acted emotionally, and were easily manipulated.

Plato believed that the state is a magnified individual and that the ideal state should be ruled by aristocrats. During the [[Middle Ages]], the [[feudal system]] dominated, but the [[Magna Carta]] limited the powers of the king and emphasized the law and representation of the people. The foundations of modern democracies were laid during the [[Age of Enlightenment]].

Since its inception, democracy has failed to realize the ideal order it promises. Today, democracy is viewed through a winner-loser lens, whereas in an ideal democracy, there are no losers—everyone is represented. Even a vote for an animal rights party is not wasted, as it ensures representation.

During [[Türkiye]]'s [[2017]] constitutional referendum, the majority of voters cast their ballots without reading the constitution. This shows how democracy has drifted from its ideals into a system shaped by leaders and voter interests. [[Ibn Khaldun]]'s theory of social cycles may help explain the crises democracy faces.

[[Robert Michels]]'s [[Iron Law of Oligarchy]] argues that every democratic system eventually turns into rule by a select elite. This results from people’s tendency to delegate their responsibilities to leaders. However, instead of abandoning democracy, we should focus on fixing its implementation. The solution lies in ideals and strategies aimed at the individual.
