# Note
I recently read [[Thinking in Systems]] by [[Donella H. Meadows]], a book that explores the concept of systems thinking. While I found the book's academic approach challenging, I'm glad I read it because it provided valuable insights and terminology that I can apply to my own life.

The book starts by defining the basic components of a system, including stocks, inflows, outflows, and feedback loops. It then explores different types of systems, including single-stock systems, renewable stock systems, and systems with delays. Meadows also discusses the importance of understanding how systems work, including their resilience, self-organization, and hierarchy.

The book also delves into why systems surprise us, highlighting the role of beguiling events, nonexistent boundaries, and mental models. It explores common system traps, such as policy resistance, the tragedy of the commons, and addiction, and offers solutions for addressing these issues.

Meadows concludes by emphasizing the importance of understanding leverage points, which are places where we can intervene in a system to create change. She cautions against making drastic changes to systems, suggesting instead that we learn to "dance with our systems" by making small, incremental adjustments.

While I found the book's academic style challenging, I appreciate the insights it provided and the terminology it introduced. I'm grateful for having read it, but I don't think it's a book I would recommend to everyone.
