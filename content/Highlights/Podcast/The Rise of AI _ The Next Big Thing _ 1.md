## Episode AI notes
1. The rise of AI began in 2012 when DeepMind, backed by Elon Musk and Larry Page, showcased an AI player that could learn and develop its own strategies in a video game called 'breakout'. This sparked interest and investments from major tech companies, leading to a cutthroat race for AI dominance.
2. AI had a period of stagnation known as 'AI winter' until the 2010s when significant computer advancements and the availability of massive datasets revived its progress.
3. Big tech companies like Google, Microsoft, and Meta view AI as crucial for the future of their businesses and are focused on developing the most powerful AI possible.
4. In the high-stakes meeting, Microsoft sought to acquire Jeff Hinton's neural network research to catch up with competitors. Eventually, they offer up to $20 million for the acquisition.
5. The founders of DNN Research had to make a decision between Google and Baidu as potential buyers. Despite a matching bid of 44 million from Baidu, they chose Google due to perceived ethics, solidifying Google's lead in AI.
6. Sophisticated AI in the game of Go, Facebook's advancements in image recognition, and Google's new computer chip for more efficient AI were key developments in the race for AI dominance.
7. The ethical concerns surrounding AI-generated images, the focus of AI research companies on potential consequences, and the importance of ethics in AI development were significant topics discussed.
