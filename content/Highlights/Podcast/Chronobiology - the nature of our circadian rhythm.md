## The Impact of Artificial Light

After we invent electric and interior lighting, people are worrying about changing our biological clocks. But it is, actually, not that problem as it is hard to replace sunlight for our body. We get around 1000 times less light inside than we do outside. It is around 100 to 400 lux inside, and 10k lux for a rainy day, and 100k lux for a sunny day.

But on the other hand, darkness is also crucial, and we are not doing well to stay in darkness at night. Even if we set colors of the light to warmer lights, it is better to get away from lights to give a clear signal to our body: It is time to sleep.

## Challenges for the Visually Impaired

For blind people, it is a lot harder to adapt their body clock to a day. Without daily light as a signal, they are eager to have hard times to adapt their own days to the world. They can easily live in 25 hours of days, which causes continuous jet lags.

There is a newly published article about that, please read the abstract of it.[2](#footnote-2)

## The Alarm Clock Dilemma

Body clock and chronobiology is mostly influenced by genetics, similar to height. It creates a bell curve over the population, so that very rare of us have a very unusual body clock. That’s why and how we can collectively decide to work from 8 to 5. But that means, some of us are collateral damage. So, he advises all of us to test ourselves if we have bizarre body clock.

But body clock is changing over ages, like our height. So, we started our life by sleeping a lot, then being a night owl for university years and then most probably living a normal life in normal hours when we became a real adult.

One easy way to understand if we are failing to adjust our body clock to society is: If you really need an alarm clock or not. So, it supposes to be kind of safety precaution for most of the days if you managed to sleep regularly. If you never get up and realizes oh it is almost the alarm clock, then you have a problem. It is most probably a problem of how you spend your days, like eating habits or insufficient exercises. But some really feel a lot productive in nighttime.

## Being an outdoor person is an easy life hack

According to our guest, and the study[3](#footnote-3) he referred, another way to find our inner peace with our inner clock is spending more time outside. He mentioned that camping at weekends is great because it forces you to adjust your sleep to nature, probably without not much light at nights and having daylight all day.

## Make our offices great again!

So, action point from that outdoor studies is: We should design our buildings (especially the ones we spend the days, a.k.a. offices) with this fact in mind. We should have a clear sight of outside during the day. It means bigger windows, using natural light instead of artificial ones. That is probably why sleeping regularly is quite easy for people working outside. They are burning more calories over a day, and they are mostly having an eye contact to sky for all day.

## Do we need to start our day exactly the same time?

With a global economy, it might be economically better :) But it has side effects.
So, it is hard to reshape the society. But imagine if every city has their own working hours tighted to their sunrise and sunset. Locally, it is totally fine. But it makes things like global finance, international businesses, harder to organize.

## Key takeaways

- Avoid using an alarm clock if possible.
- Spend lunchtime outside, if you are an office-worker.
- Sleep a bit more than weekdays in weekends if you had a busy week with less sleep than usual.
- Don’t use screens before sleep.
- Better office means more daylight, more natural air while you are working.
