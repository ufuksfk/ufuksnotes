In this episode of [Hardware to Save a Planet](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/hardware-to-save-a-planet/id1628554388), host Dylan Garrett sits down with [Curtis VanWalleghem](https://www.linkedin.com/in/curtisvanwalleghem/), Co-Founder and CEO of [[Hydrostor]], to discuss how compressed air energy storage can revolutionize renewable energy. Hydrostor's innovative underground caverns store air and water to provide long-duration, cost-effective energy storage—solving the intermittency challenge in renewable grids. The conversation dives into engineering breakthroughs, economic advantages over batteries, and the policy shifts needed to enable 100% renewable energy.
# Note
 Hydrostor offers a novel approach to utility-scale compressed air energy storage, providing a solution to balance the intermittent supply of renewable energy from wind and solar.
The company uses compressed air and thermal energy storage, with air stored in mined caverns and heat stored in hot water, offering a long-life, robust system for energy storage.
Hydrostor's technology is particularly cost-effective for longer durations of energy storage, making it a competitive alternative to lithium-ion batteries and pumped hydro.
The company is developing large-scale projects, such as a 200 MW project in Australia and a 500 MW project in California, which can significantly impact regional energy grids.
Hydrostor's system provides synchronous inertia, a critical grid reliability service that batteries cannot offer, making it valuable in renewable-heavy markets.
The company aims to capture a significant share of the global energy storage market, particularly in the 8 to 24-hour storage duration segment.
Hydrostor's business model includes both developing and operating storage projects and licensing its technology to utilities, with a focus on building a track record of successful projects.
The company emphasizes the importance of market redesign to support the integration of renewable energy and storage solutions, advocating for policy changes to facilitate this transition.
