# Note
 Attention spans are shrinking, but that doesn't necessarily mean we're losing our ability to focus. We're simply paying attention to different things, and often in more intense ways. The idea that we should strive for long, extended hours of focus is a myth. Our cognitive resources are limited, and we have rhythms of focused attention that correspond to the ebb and flow of those resources.

The craving for certainty in uncertain times is dangerous. Our information environment is chaotic, and we're bombarded with misinformation. This has led to the commodification of certainty, with science being used to sell everything from shampoo to ideologies. We need to embrace scientific humility and recognize that science is messy and uncertain. We also need to develop our critical thinking skills and take the time to reflect on the information we consume.

The postmodernist idea that all knowledge is relative has flipped, and it's now being embraced by the right. This is a dangerous shift, as it undermines the foundations of knowledge. However, there is hope. We're learning more about misinformation and how to counter it. There's also a growing army of science communicators who are working to keep the candle of science burning bright.
