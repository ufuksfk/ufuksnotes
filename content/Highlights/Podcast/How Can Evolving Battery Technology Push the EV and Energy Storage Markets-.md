# Note
 Battery technology is constantly evolving, with new chemistries and form factors emerging to improve energy density, safety, charging speed, and longevity.
Lithium sulfur batteries are a promising technology due to their high energy density and abundance of materials, but they are still in the early stages of industrialization.
Solid state batteries offer significant safety advantages by eliminating the risk of thermal runaway, but they are challenging to manufacture at scale.
Sodium ion batteries are a safer alternative to lithium ion batteries, but they have lower energy density and are better suited for applications like golf carts and small vehicles.
The battery industry is facing challenges in sustainably sourcing materials, but recycling and ethical mining practices are becoming increasingly important.
The EU battery passport is a policy initiative that aims to promote transparency and sustainability in the battery supply chain, and other countries are considering similar legislation.
The future of battery technology is bright, with potential for significant advancements in energy density, charging speed, and safety, leading to a wider range of applications and a more sustainable future.
