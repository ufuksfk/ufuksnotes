# Note
 I'm Nihal Arthur Nayaker, and this is a brief history of Stuff, a podcast about the ordinary objects around us. Today, we're talking about vacuum cleaners, inspired by the Science Museum Group collection.
 

Kate Perks, Senior Collections Care Conservator at the Science Museum, explains how they care for objects, including cleaning them with microfiber cloths and vacuum cleaners. The museum has four conservation assistants who clean all the objects on open display every day.

Laura Humphries, a carpet sweeper enthusiast and author of a book about housework, joins us to discuss the history of the vacuum cleaner. She explains that the story begins with the broom, then moves to the carpet sweeper, and finally to the vacuum cleaner. The vacuum cleaner revolutionized how we clean carpets and changed the way houses look.

The first vacuum cleaners were massive, horse-drawn machines, like the one in the Science Museum collection. Hubert Cecil Booth, the man who brought vacuum cleaners to the masses, designed these huge machines. He was inspired by a demonstration of a machine that blew dirt away and thought, "What if it sucked the dirt into a box?"

The vacuum cleaner became a status symbol, and companies like Hoover marketed their machines to housewives. The Hoover factory in Greenford, London, is a beautiful art deco building that used to build and test all models of Hoovers.

The basic technology of the vacuum cleaner hasn't changed much since Booth's time. It still sucks up dirt and puts it into a receptacle. However, vacuum cleaners have gotten smaller and smarter, with robotic vacuum cleaners now available.

Housework has always been a gendered activity, with women often bearing the brunt of the work. Despite advances in technology, women still spend a comparable amount of time on housework as they did a century ago. This is because standards have risen, and we now expect everything to be spotless.

Laura believes that housework is fundamentally a bit rubbish, but new technology can improve it. She's excited to see how smart technology will develop and how people will hack domestic tech.

Kate is intrigued by how new technologies, like the phone, have changed our lives and how they will change the way we clean our houses in the future. She hopes for a more equitable share of housework in the future.
