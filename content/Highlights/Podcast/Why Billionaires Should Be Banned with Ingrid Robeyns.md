[[Ingrid Robeyns]] [[Limitarianism The Case Against Extreme Wealth]]
# Note
 I argue that it is not just bad to have billionaires, but that it is bad to have deca-millionaires, people who have more than 10 million euros, pounds, or dollars. I am a professor of political philosophy and have been working on questions of inequality for the last 20 years. I believe that we should increasingly worry about wealth and wealth concentration.

I make several arguments against extreme wealth. One is that the money is often earned in a way that makes it morally questionable. Another is that too much wealth harms societies, either by undermining democracy or by being incompatible with principles of ecological sustainability. I also argue that no one can say they deserve to be super rich in a moral sense. Finally, I argue that it is not good for the super rich themselves because it does not make them happy.

I believe that luck plays a central role in our lives and that we should be generous towards those who were less lucky. I argue that billionaires and people who are over deca-millionaires are bad for the rest of society, even if one were to think they deserve it. I believe that this sort of concentration of wealth undermines democracy by giving the wealthy an unfair amount of political power. I also argue that the super rich are able to live in a fantasy world where their dependency on others is so abstracted away from them that they do not see the people who are actually dependent on them for their wealth.
