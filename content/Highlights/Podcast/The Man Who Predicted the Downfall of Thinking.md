# Note
 [[Neil Postman]], a media theorist, argued that people will come to adore technologies that undo their capacity to think, citing [[Aldous Huxley]]'s prediction that people would be overwhelmed by information and become passive.
Postman believed that television, and later, the internet and social media, have transformed our culture from a typographic one to one dominated by entertainment and attention-grabbing content.
He argued that the shift from a typographic culture to a televised one has negatively impacted politics, religion, and education, as these institutions have become more focused on entertainment and spectacle.
Postman's concept of "technopoly" describes a society where technology becomes an end in itself, driving innovation for the sake of innovation, and where efficiency is valued above all else.
Postman proposed seven questions to ask about any new technology, including what problem it solves, whose problem it is, what new problems it creates, and what changes in language it promotes.
