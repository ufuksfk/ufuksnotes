# Note
 About a year and a half ago, we published an episode called "Is Google Getting Worse?" We discussed Google's near monopoly over internet search and looked at a startup called Niva that was trying to compete. Since then, a new kind of competitor has emerged: search engines powered by artificial intelligence. One such engine, called Perplexity, is a conversational answer engine that provides answers and cites its sources.

Perplexity's CEO, Aravind Srinivas, believes that AI-powered search engines will not be a monopoly, as there are already several players in the market, including OpenAI, Meta, Microsoft, and Apple. He argues that Google could have been the monopoly but was too slow to develop its own AI chatbot. Perplexity offers a free version of its product but also a premium version for $20 a month, which provides a better service.

We then revisited the original "Is Google Getting Worse?" episode, which explored how Google came to dominate web search and the potential downsides of its monopoly. Google's search engine was built on a clever algorithm called PageRank, which ranked search results based on the number of links pointing to a given web page. Google's success was also driven by its innovative ad tech system, which auctioned off the first few listings on search results based on factors like the advertiser's bid and the relevance of the ad.
