[[Signal]]
# Note
 The Signalgate scandal, a reference to the Watergate scandal, involves a secure messaging app used for high-level government discussions. The app, while secure, is ultimately a third-party application, leaving it vulnerable to hacking, particularly phishing attacks. This vulnerability is exacerbated by the users' preference for convenience over security protocols.

Official communications should be archived for legal and historical reasons, but this chat group was designed to self-destruct after a week. This disregard for protocol, coupled with the sharing of sensitive operational details by the Defense Secretary, highlights a significant security breach.

A journalist was mistakenly added to the group, witnessing the discussions and subsequent missile strikes. The journalist's initial skepticism gave way to the realization of the administration's incompetence. The discussions revealed a shocking lack of foresight and concern for civilian casualties, prioritizing European interests over human lives and strategic considerations.

The administration's response to the scandal was far from ideal. Instead of acknowledging the mistakes, they resorted to personal attacks and smear campaigns against the journalist. Senate hearings proved to be performative rather than genuinely investigative, with officials offering evasive answers. The lack of accountability and the president's refusal to admit fault are characteristic of his administration.

Further embarrassments emerged, including the revelation of publicly accessible personal information of high-ranking officials, highlighting their technological incompetence and vulnerability to further attacks. The incident underscores the administration's consistent pattern of incompetence and disregard for security protocols. The entire situation is absurd, revealing a leadership that is both incompetent and seemingly unconcerned with the consequences of their actions.
