# Note
 This week's episode discusses viewing cinema through political or moral lenses. We often judge a film's quality based on how well it aligns with our own beliefs, a trap we should avoid.

Historically, art was controlled by central authorities, reflecting elite tastes. Modern art broke free, becoming more individualistic and critical. However, even with this freedom, mainstream cinema, especially Hollywood, often reflects general moral values.

While more diverse viewpoints are now expressed, we shouldn't judge films solely on whether we agree with their politics or morals. Even highly influential movements like Soviet cinema, initially groundbreaking, later became restrictive, demonstrating the inherent contradictions in using such criteria.

The success of morally ambiguous characters like Joker and Thanos, and the popularity of films like Scream 6, highlight the complexities of audience reception and the fluidity of moral judgments across time and cultures. We should evaluate films based on their cinematic merit, not solely their political or moral stances. My own approach is to judge films based on their technical aspects, regardless of their message.

Neden? Çünkü o sinemaya politik bakıyor, komünizme en iyi hizmet eden filmlerin en iyi filmler olduğunu düşünüyor. Ama ben sinema tarihine gösterge bilimsel bakıyorum, o dönemi değerli görmüyorum.
