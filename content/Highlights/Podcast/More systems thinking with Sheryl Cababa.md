# Note
I've been touring the world since my book, [[Closing the Loop Systems Thinking for Designers]], was published, giving talks and workshops. I've learned that many designers, especially junior ones, struggle to apply systems thinking in their work. They feel limited by their role and lack of power within their organizations. This surprised me because I've always found systems thinking to be a natural part of my work as a design strategist.

I think this is because systems thinking is a mindset, a way of looking at the world. It's about understanding the underlying structures and mental models that influence the things we see. It's not just about designing screens or features, but about understanding the broader context and impact of our work. Even if you're working on a single feature, it's important to consider the larger system in which it operates.

I'm disappointed that many designers feel powerless to make a difference. They want to have a positive impact on the world, but they feel limited by their role and lack of power. I think this is a systemic problem, and it's something that we need to address as a design community. We need to empower designers to think critically and strategically, and to understand the broader context of their work. We need to teach them how to use systems thinking to identify leverage points and make a real difference.
