# Note
 There are many anecdotal complaints about Google search not being what it used to be. To test this claim, we looked at product reviews and found that Google performs the best at filtering out spammy websites without much content. However, certain legacy magazine websites have sections that churn out a lot of pages every day just to get advertising and link commission money, and these websites tend to get ranked highly on Google. Google issues periodic updates to their search algorithms to push low quality review websites off the results page, but these websites learn the new ways to game the search engine and creep back up again. This is a constant game of cat and mouse between content publishers and Google.

Google's chief scientist for search, Pandu Nayak, confirms that there is a constant need to update search results to keep them relevant. Google has added all kinds of additional measures of whether a page is high or low quality, and they have a set of spam policies that allows them to take action against sites that are trying to manipulate them. Google had a big update in March aimed at improving search quality, and they are always updating their search tools, even using artificial intelligence. Google's AI overview, which generates answers to users' queries using a large language model, is not always accurate and has occasionally spawned some unintentionally hilarious answers.

Generative AI makes it easier to create a kind of junk website, but Google is ready and willing to engage with that problem. The computer scientist who investigated Google search, Mati Wiegman, is feeling optimistic about the future of search because of all the extra attention from academics and government into how big tech operates. He eventually found his messenger bag using Google, and he respects the research that went into finding it.
