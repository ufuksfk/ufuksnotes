## Marie-Claude Dubois journey

She started her career around 90’s as a researcher and main problem she had been trying to solve is how to maintain daylight in offices without overheating the office from the sunlight.

She started her career in Quebec, Canada. Back in those days, energy was almost free there, and everyone questioned her why she is trying to reduce energy consumption in buildings. Once she moved to Sweden, she realized energy prices are a lot higher here and there had been already established energy efficiency culture.
### Passive Houses in Sweden

She also mentioned her latest PhD projects with her students, mainly focused on daylight in houses and workplaces. Daylight is basically a free source of energy. So instead of cultivating that light, why we created that bizarre cities with more than 10 floors where entry floors had basically no daylight. It was an effect of urbanization, as we need dense cities, they found that solution to increase the height of buildings. And she felt we are very lucky in Sweden, compared to Canada.

Daylight is also very important for hygiene and public health. It is a natural killer for indoor bacterias.

### Lighting in Schools and Offices

They started to discuss the importance of lighting in schools. She referred a couple of researches about it. One is from 90s in the USA, they did study over 20 thousand children and found that students from schools with better daylight perform a lot better at mathematics and reading.[1](#footnote-1)

In other studies, they also found a correlation with hormonal levels, melatonin for example. Also, growth of the children was affected by light.

By the normal design of a typical classroom, we have a problem of distributing daylight over the room. They can get a lot better daylight closer to the windows. So she suggested designing a classroom with multiple daylight sources, preferably from different perspective. Uniformity of the light is quite important, and to measure the performance of the lighting we should check the median of daylight, which means half of the room is lighter than that value, and the other half is darker. But if you get average in simulations, it is risky information as it can mean just a couple of students near the window can get very good light, while others stays in dark :) It is also same for all kind of living places. But classrooms and offices are quite important as most of us spending our life there.

### Lighting in Homes

For our homes, we should also regulate the size and number of windows. It is more health problem as it affects our circadian rhythm. And more daylight together with a natural air ventilation means a better indoor air quality.

If you need to sacrifice a couple of rooms, it is generally corridors, toilets, storage rooms and bedroom. But bedroom should be the upper half of the median, together with kitchen and living room.

### Lux at walls, lux at desks

We have an old tradition to measure the lux performance within horizontal surfaces, as we generally had just a desk, paper and pen to work before technology era. But, she mentioned we should focus on more vertical performance of the light, as we are barely looking to the ceiling or ground, but mostly to the front. And all the computer screens and whiteboards are also in vertical direction.

To summarize this episode, remember: the sun rises every day for us. Instead of harnessing its light for our living spaces, we often rely on electricity throughout the day. This approach is neither natural, environmentally friendly, nor healthy.

However, simply suggesting we replace walls with windows isn't practical. We must find a balance between heat insulation, cost, public health, and sustainability.
