[[Ingvar Kamprad]] and [[IKEA]]
# Note
 Ingvar Kamprad, IKEA's founder, was a complex figure. His business acumen was undeniable, starting with selling matches at age five and building IKEA into a global furniture giant. His innovative flat-pack furniture and low prices revolutionized the industry.

Kamprad's frugality was extreme, bordering on miserliness. He drove an old Volvo, flew economy, and lived modestly, even while amassing a vast fortune. This was presented as both a genuine lifestyle choice and a potentially performative act.

However, Kamprad's past held darker elements. He was a member of Sweden's fascist movement in his youth and maintained a close friendship with a known Nazi and Holocaust denier. While he later apologized for his youthful mistakes, his actions and lack of significant reparations cast a shadow on his legacy.

The panel unanimously deemed Kamprad "evil," citing his Nazi affiliations as unforgivable, despite his business success and the accessibility he brought to affordable furniture. His tax avoidance and the use of East German political prisoners in his production further fueled this judgment.
