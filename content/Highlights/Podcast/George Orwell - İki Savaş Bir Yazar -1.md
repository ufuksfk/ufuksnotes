# Note
 This episode focuses on George Orwell, exploring his life and works, particularly within the context of interwar Europe. My co-host and I, while not experts, are enthusiastic about Orwell and his relevance to our times. We began this project partly to understand Orwell's life and how it shaped his writing.

My introduction to Orwell was through a Disney cartoon adaptation of Animal Farm, followed by excerpts from Nineteen Eighty-Four in high school. Later, a book about European cities piqued my interest in his Homage to Catalonia, which profoundly impacted me. Subsequently, I read more of his works, including Burmese Days, Down and Out in Paris and London, and biographies of Orwell and his wife, Eileen. I find studying Orwell's biography alongside his novels incredibly insightful, revealing the context and evolution of his writing.

My co-host's initial encounter with Orwell was through Nineteen Eighty-Four, found in his father's library, a book that significantly impacted him due to its dystopian vision. He also read Animal Farm in high school, appreciating its allegorical nature. Later, he explored Orwell's other works, including Aspidistra and Coming Up for Air, finding them resonant with his own experiences. He also read Orwell's essays, discovering a nuanced perspective on freedom of thought and the complexities of socialism.

We discussed Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four, written while he was terminally ill, and its enduring impact. We also examined Animal Farm, its initial difficulty in publication, and its later success, partly due to the end of World War II. We touched upon Orwell's complex relationship with the socialist movement and his criticisms of both Stalinism and the British Communist Party.

Orwell's Homage to Catalonia, co-written with his wife Eileen, is another key work we discussed, highlighting its modest initial sales and later popularity in America. We also explored the circumstances surrounding the title of Nineteen Eighty-Four, including a possible connection to Eileen's earlier poem of the same name. Finally, we noted Orwell's work for the BBC during World War II, including his unusual payment of royalties to Hitler. We concluded by mentioning a statue of Orwell outside the BBC building, a point of irony given his complex relationship with the organization.
