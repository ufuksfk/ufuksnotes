# Note
 Marty Cagan focuses on building strong cross-functional product teams, believing that products come from teams, not just product managers.
Traditional product management, in Cagan's view, is good product management, while the rest is "crappy" and often involves splitting product management into three orthogonal roles: research and design, technical project management, and product marketing.
Cagan advocates for empowered product teams, which are given problems to solve rather than features to build, allowing them to leverage the brains of their members and iterate on solutions.
He distinguishes between empowerment, autonomy, and self-management, emphasizing that empowerment allows teams to figure out the best way to solve a problem, while autonomy means they have everything they need to make that change.
Leaders should provide context to empowered teams, including product vision and strategy, and assign problems to solve, rather than dictating solutions. They are also responsible for staffing, coaching, and team topology.
