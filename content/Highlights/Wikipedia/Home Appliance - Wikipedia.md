## Highlights
- The domestic application attached to home appliance is tied to the definition of appliance as "an instrument or device designed for a particular use or function".[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_appliance/#cite_note-3) *[Collins English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_English_Dictionary)* defines "home appliance" as: "devices or machines, usually electrical, that are in your home and which you use to do jobs such as cleaning or cooking".[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_appliance/#cite_note-4) The broad usage allows for nearly any device intended for domestic use to be a home appliance, including [consumer electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_electronics) as well as [stoves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stove),[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_appliance/#cite_note-Bulletin_2014-5) [refrigerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigerator), [toasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toaster)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_appliance/#cite_note-Bulletin_2014-5) and [air conditioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioner).
- The industry saw significant growth post-World War II, with the introduction of [dishwashers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dishwasher) and [clothes dryers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothes_dryer).
- By the [[1980s]], the appliance industry was booming, leading to mergers and antitrust legislation
- By the [[1990s]], five companies dominated over 90% of the market
- [Major appliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_appliance), often called [white goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_goods), include items like refrigerators and washing machines, while [small appliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_appliance) encompass items such as [toasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toaster) and [coffee makers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_maker).[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_appliance/#cite_note-6) [[White goods]]
- Product design shifted in the [[1960s]], embracing new materials and colors.
- [[consumer electronics]]often referred to as [[brown goods]] include items like [[Television]] and computers.
- The invention of Earl Richardson's small electric [[Clothes Iron]] in [[1903]] gave a small initial boost to the home appliance industry
- In America during the [[1980s]], the industry shipped $1.5 billion worth of goods each year and employed over 14,000 workers, with revenues doubling between [[1982]] and [[1990]] to $3.3 billion
- In the [[1990s]], the appliance industry was very consolidated, with over 90% of the products being sold by just five companies. For example, in [[1991]], dishwasher manufacturing market share was split between [[General Electric]] with 40% market share, [[Whirlpool]] with 31%, [[Electrolux]] with 20%, [Maytag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maytag) with 7% and [Thermador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermador) with just 2%
- In the [[1960s]] the[[Product Design]] for appliances such as [washing machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washing_machine), [[Refrigerator]] ,and [electric toasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toaster) shifted away from [[Streamline Moderne]] and embraced technological advances in the fabrication of [sheet metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheet_metal). A choice in color, as well as [fashionable accessory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_accessory), could be offered to the [mass market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_market) without increasing [production cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_cost). Home appliances were sold as space-saving ensembles
- In British English, they are often called "[[brown goods]]" by producers and sellers, to distinguish them from "[[White goods]]" which are meant for [housekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housekeeping) tasks, such as washing machines and refrigerators, although nowadays, these could be considered brown goods, some of these being connected to the Internet.
- There is a trend of networking home appliances together, and combining their controls and key functions. For instance, energy distribution could be managed more evenly so that when a [[washing machine]] is on, an [[oven]] can go into a delayed start mode, or vice versa [[Home automation]]
