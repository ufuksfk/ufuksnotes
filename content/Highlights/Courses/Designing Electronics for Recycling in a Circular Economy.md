Shredding vs. Dismantling

The fact that shredding is (by far) the most likely recycling scenario has clear implications for design. Of course, your product may need to be disassembled to some extent for other purposes, such as repair or maintenance. But when you are considering the product’s end-of-life, it is most beneficial to focus your design efforts on how effectively the product can be shredded.

avoid using adhesives.

- Connection types like snap fits, hooks, and turn locks separate more easily during shredding, facilitating recyclability.
- Components glued together, such as LED panels on reflective sheets, tend to stay attached, resulting in significant material loss and contamination.
- Tape often remains attached to materials
- Magnetic connections may lead to ferrite fragments contaminating ferrous metals.
- Inserts fully enclosed in materials, like metal inserts in plastic, are more likely to stay attached during shredding, affecting how well materials can be sorted.
- Thoughtful design choices are needed, like minimizing adhesives and selecting easy-to-separate connectors, facilitate material liberation and reduce contamination in recycling streams.
- Using materials that can be processed in the same material stream or applying connectors for easy breakdown of materials that require different processing in different material streams improves the likelihood of effective separation during recycling.

## Design Guidelines for EEE
product, component and material level.
* reduce complexity by minimising total number of components
* modularity
* easy to open
* avoid creating permanent connections, consider using click fingers
* avoid welding insert moulding
* minimise cable number and length
* use recyclable or recycled plastic
* minimise the number of different materials used
* avoid mixing materials e.g. through 2K moulding
* avoid harmful materials, substances and additives
