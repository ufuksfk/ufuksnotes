[[Inside view vs Outside view]]: [[Daniel Kahneman]]
We take inside view when we make judgements based on narrowly details of particular project.
Our natural inclination to inside view can be defeated by following analogies to outside view.
Outside view is counterintiuitive because it requires a decision maker to ignore surface features of the current project, on which they are expert. And instead of look outside for structurally similar analogies, it requires a mindset switch from narrow to broad.
A study about investors:
The investors initially judged their own projects. where they knew all the details, completely differently from similar projects to which they were outsiders.
This is widespread phenomenon. If you're asked to predict whether a particular horse will win a race or particular politician will win an election, the more internal details you learn about any particular scenario, the more likely you are to say that the scenario you are investigating will occur.
Focusing narrowly on many fine details spesific to a problem feels like the exact thing to do, while it is opposite.
