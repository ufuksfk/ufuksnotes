## Highlights
- Lighting the Path to the Future Picture [[New York]] City in near-total darkness, a city of millions stumbling through dark streets lit only by the moon and stars. New York City's lamplighters are now largely forgotten, but before electrification, they were the unsung heroes who ensured that streets remained navigable after sunset. Each worker was responsible for lighting and maintaining about 50 oil lamps, making their rounds each night. In [[1907]], the lamplighters staged a strike, plunging New York into an eerie darkness. - Throughout history, technology has been the single most important driver behind economic and social change.
- Economists estimate that over 80% of the income differences between rich and poor countries can be explained by differential rates of technology adoption. [[economy]]
- Desperate workers, most famously the Luddites, smashed machines in protest. These workers clashed with the British Army and the rising class of industrialists who embraced mechanisation. The industrialists won, but their victory wasn't inevitable.
- Many European cities prohibited automatic looms in the [[17th century]] to prevent worker unrest.
- [[China]], well into the [[19th century]], imported sewing machines were destroyed by workers fearful of losing their jobs.
- Imagine an era where blue-collar factory workers steadily achieve middle-class lifestyles on the basis of their wages alone. A time when even high school graduates could expect to find secure, decent-paying jobs. ... It describes the lived reality of the American workforce for much of the period between [[1870]] and [[1970]], an era economists have dubbed the Great Levelling.
- Rather than simply automating workers' jobs, these innovations spawned completely new industries and occupations.
- Yet a major driver of shared prosperity in [[20th century]] America was the productive interplay between technological progress and education.
- Drawing on Nobel Prize-winning economist Jan Tinbergen, the author suggests that when the race between technology and education stays neck-and-neck, broadly shared prosperity is the natural result.
- other factors, like unionisation, the World Wars and the Great Depression, also played supporting roles in the great levelling.

- Yet this heyday of inclusive growth offers no ironclad guarantees for the future. The trajectory of social inequality hinges on a kind of race, a race between the demand for skills and their supply.
