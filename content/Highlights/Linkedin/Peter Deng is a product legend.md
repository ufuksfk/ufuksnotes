[[Peter Deng]]

[[Product Management]]
he was the first PM at [[Facebook]] and VP at [[OpenAI]].

Here are his 16 lessons on building products:  
It is actually his notes from [[Lenny's Podcast: Product]] episode with 
  
1/ Product teams need 5 PM archetypes: consumer (half designer), growth (half data scientist), business (half MBA), platform (builds tools), and research (half researcher).  
	I am a bit  consumer, a bit researcher.
  
2/ [[Facebook]] succeeded because they understood people's fundamental desire to connect. Build products that work with human nature, not against it.  
  
3/ Use the [[IDEO]] framework: Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test. Most PMs skip straight to solutions without understanding the problem.  
  
4/ You must personally experience your users' problems – no summary or report can replace sitting in user research sessions and hearing their frustration firsthand.  [[(UX) User Experience]]
  
5/ AI products succeed by fitting into existing workflows seamlessly, not by having the smartest model. Cursor beats GitHub copilot with better user experience, not with better AI.  
  
6/ In the AI era, your competitive advantage isn't the model – it's your unique data and flywheel to keep generating it. Unique data that improves over time beats distribution advantages.  
  
7/ Forget DAUs and volume – track cohorted retention. If the retention curve doesn't level off, you don't have product-market fit.  
  
8/ At [[Uber]], the price and ETA was the real product – not the pixels on screen. Focus on what users fundamentally care about, not just your beautiful UI.  
  
9/ When scaling 0→1B users, you must build systems that let you go sustainably faster. Sometimes you go slow to go fast.  
  
10/ Building a growth team doesn't just drive growth – it forces you to properly measure your product and ask the right questions about user behavior.  
  
11/ Obsess over craft details while having wisdom about which details don't actually matter. Balance deep caring with business perspective on where to spend time.  
  
12/ "We may not be right, but at least we're not confused." Indecision kills more products than bad decisions.  
  
13/ AGI won't solve everything automatically. Like databases needed builders to create value, AGI needs product people to channel intelligence into useful applications.  
  
14/ Change roles every 2-3 years to learn different skills. Peter regularly moved to new challenges to build diverse product knowledge across different industries.  
  
15/ Set up teams like an RPG where everyone has different superpowers. Create balance by having people who spike in different areas and obsess over different things.  
  
16/ Ask candidates about their biggest mistakes and how it changed them. You can easily tell who's being genuine and who’s not.  
  
This is a recap from [[Lenny's Podcast: Product]] podcast. It’s a masterclass in both product building and product leadership.
