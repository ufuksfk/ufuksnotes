It’s not just [[Apple]]. There is a global network of semiconductor suppliers building the components that power it—from the logic board to memory to RF.  
  
This teardown of the iPhone 15 Pro reveals the key chip suppliers:  
-> Logic Board  
[[Broadcom]] – Wireless charging receiver  
[[Texas Instruments]] – USB interface & PMIC  
SK hynix – 8GB DRAM  
Cirrus Logic – Audio codec & amplifier  
Apple (in-house) – A17 Pro processor  
[[Renesas]] – Power management ICs  
[[STMicroelectronics]] – Power management ICs  
[[Bosch]] – MEMS accelerometer & gyroscope  
  
-> Memory Board  
Kioxia – 256GB NAND flash memory  
Texas Instruments – AMOLED display power supply  
Cirrus Logic – Audio amplifier  
[[NXP]] – NFC controller & secure element  
  
-> Radio Frequency Board  
[[Qualcomm]] – 5G modem & RF transceivers  
Broadcom – RF front-end modules  
Skyworks & Qorvo – RF filters and amplifiers  
NXP – NFC controller  
STMicroelectronics – Secure MCU  
  
Key takeaway:  
No smartphone is truly built by one company. Your iPhone 15 is a collaboration between over 10 global chip suppliers, each specializing in RF, memory, power management, or logic. From Broadcom’s wireless chips to SK hynix’s memory and Qualcomm’s modems, it’s a complex supply chain that makes the device work.
