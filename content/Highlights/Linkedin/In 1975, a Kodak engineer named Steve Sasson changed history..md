In [[1975]], a [[Kodak]] engineer named [[Steve Sasson]] changed history.  
  
He built the world’s first digital camera.  [[History of Technology]]
[[Kodak DCS]]
  
No film. No moving parts.  
  
Just a CCD image sensor + analog-to-digital converter + embedded logic that stored the photo in memory.  
  
Specs:  
  
0.01 megapixels  
23 seconds to capture + process one image  
The photo was displayed on a TV screen. No print, no negative.  
  
At the time, no one took it seriously.  
  
(Afterall, would want to look at pictures on a screen?)  
  
But that tiny embedded vision prototype quietly sparked a revolution.  
  
It paved the way for digital photography.  
It shaped the entire smartphone camera industry.  
It changed how we document, share, and remember life itself.  
  
All thanks to embedded systems turning light into data, and data into memories.  
  
Today, embedded vision is everywhere:  
  
- Smart cameras in factories  
- Face recognition on your phone  
- Drones, AR headsets, medical imaging  
  
Every groundbreaking innovation starts with a small, bold step.
