## Highlights
- “The Jetsons” cartoon. The [first episode of The Jetsons’ first season](https://thejetsons.fandom.com/wiki/Rosey_the_Robot_(episode)) in [[1962]] was even called “Rosey the Robot.”
- the first robot vacuum that looks and functions as we know them now was released in 1996.
- The first patented robot vacuum concept (1956)
- Not long after Moore’s patent, the **RCA Whirlpool Miracle Kitchen** was on display in Moscow at the height of the Cold War, in July of [[1959]]. It was memorialized as the scene of a historic exchange between Soviet Premier Nikita Khrushchev and [[Richard Nixon]], then the Vice President of the United States. Their exchange and the press that followed exemplified the philosophical differences between styles of government the two nations held.
- ![1950s Robot Vacuum Prototype - RCA-Whirlpool Miracle Kitchen](https://vacuumwars.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/1950s-Robot-Vacuum-Prototype-RCA-Whirlpool-Miracle-Kitchen-774x1024.jpeg)
- [[1950s]] Robot Vacuum Prototype from the RCA-Whirlpool Miracle Kitchen.
- iRobot Roomba (2002)The first commercially successful robot vacuum
- For example, the Roomba could **detect the presence of dirt** on the floor, and it had a **cliff sensor**, also known as a **drop sensor**, which prevented it from driving off staircases or landings between floors.
- [[Dyson]] DC06 (2000) and 360 Eye (2014)
- Dyson 360 Eye, and this one did make it to the general public. Its name came from its two sensors, the 360-degree panoramic vision sensor and the infrared (IR) distance sensor. Those two sensors took care of the robot’s navigation, employing a **systematic cleaning pattern**.
- 2010
  Neato Robotics launches the XV-11, the first robot vacuum with laser-based mapping.
- The robot vacuum market is expected to grow to [$7.83 billion by 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/en/news-release/2022/09/02/2509234/28124/en/Robotic-Vacuum-Cleaners-Global-Market-Report-2022-6-9-Annual-Growth-Driven-by-Key-Players-Dyson-LG-Panasonic-Others.html) with a compound annual growth rate of 6.9%. That number is up from $5.59 billion in 2021. As brands vie for a piece of the growing market, we expect innovation to continue to grow.
