## Highlights
- He found little commercial success until his [alternative history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative%5Fhistory "Alternative history") novel _[The Man in the High Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The%5FMan%5Fin%5Fthe%5FHigh%5FCastle "The Man in the High Castle")_ (1962) earned him acclaim, including a [Hugo Award for Best Novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo%5FAward%5Ffor%5FBest%5FNovel "Hugo Award for Best Novel"), when he was 33.
- Through his studies in philosophy, he believed that existence is based on internal human perception, which does not necessarily correspond to external reality. He described himself as "an acosmic [panentheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panentheism "Panentheism")", which he explained as meaning that "I don't believe that the universe exists. I believe that the only thing that exists is God and he is more than the universe. The universe is an extension of God into space and time. That's the premise I start from in my work, that so-called "reality" is a mass delusion that we've all been required to believe for reasons totally obscure". After reading the works of [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato") and pondering the possibilities of [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics") realms, he came to the conclusion that, in a certain sense, the world is not entirely real and there is no way to confirm whether it is truly there. That question was a theme in many of his novels.
