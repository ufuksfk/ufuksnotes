Wiki page of [[The Cult of the Amateur]]
## Highlights
- The book was based in part on a controversial essay Keen wrote for *[The Weekly Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weekly_Standard)*, criticizing Web 2.0 for being similar to [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism), for destroying professionalism and for making it impossible to find high quality material amidst all of the user-generated web content.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cult_of_the_Amateur#cite_note-NYT1-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cult_of_the_Amateur#cite_note-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cult_of_the_Amateur#cite_note-Reuters-3)
- Keen argues against the idea of a [read-write culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remix_culture) in media, stating that "most of the content being shared – no matter how many times it has been linked, cross-linked, annotated, and copied – was composed or written by someone from the sweat of their creative brow and the disciplined use of their talent." As such, he contrasts companies such as [Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Warner) and [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney) that "create and produce movies, music, magazines, and television" with companies such as [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google). He calls the latter "a parasite" since "it creates no content of its own" and "in terms of value creation, there is nothing there apart from its links."[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cult_of_the_Amateur#cite_note-Lessig-4)
- He elaborates on the point by saying, "Of course, every free listing on [Craigslist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigslist) means one less paid listing in a local newspaper. Every visit to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia)'s free information hive means one less customer for a professionally researched and edited encyclopedia such as *[Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica)*." Thus, he concludes that "what is free is actually costing us a fortune." He also refers to changes such as downsizing of newspaper business and the closing of [record labels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_label) as forms of economic loss caused by internet-based social changes.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cult_of_the_Amateur#cite_note-Lessig-4)
- "What you may not realize is that what is free is actually costing us a fortune," Mr. Keen writes. “The new winners – Google, [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube), [MySpace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MySpace), Craigslist, and the hundreds of start-ups hungry for a piece of the Web 2.0 pie – are unlikely to fill the shoes of the industries they are helping to undermine, in terms of products produced, jobs created, revenue generated or benefits conferred. By stealing away our eyeballs, the blogs and wikis are decimating the publishing, music and news-gathering industries that created the original content those Web sites 'aggregate.' Our culture is essentially cannibalizing its young, destroying the very sources of the content they crave."[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cult_of_the_Amateur#cite_note-NYT1-1)
- [Larry Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Sanger), co-founder of Wikipedia and founder of the expert-centered wiki [Citizendium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizendium), gave the book a mixed review. Sanger said that "The book is provocative, but its argument is unfortunately weakened by the fact that Keen is so over-the-top and presents more of a caricature of a position than carefully reasoned discourse."
- Dariusz Jemielniak and Aleksandra Przegalinska noted that Keen failed to distinguish between for-profit and non-profit aspects of the amateur participation ([platform capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_capitalism) represented by companies such as [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) or [Airbnb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbnb) vs [commons-based peer production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commons-based_peer_production) represented by communities such as Wikipedia or Linux).
