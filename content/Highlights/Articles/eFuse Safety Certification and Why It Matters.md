## Highlights
- Today there are numerous standards that dictate product safety in the world of electronics. Twoorganizations that define and audit companies’compliance to these safety standards are UnderwritersLaboratories (UL) and International ElectrotechnicalCommission (IEC).
- One simple way to design your product with the ULMark in mind is by selecting components that havealready been assessed by UL. Designing with UL Recognized components can help prevent long debug cycles that could result from failing UL evaluation
- Specifically, TI’s electronic fuses(eFuses) portfolio are robust power path protectiondevices that are UL 2367 Recognized and IEC 62368-1 Certified
- An eFuse embeds variousfunctions to protect a system against inrush current,overcurrent, input overvoltage, reverse current
- Unlike discretefuses and PTCs, an eFuse has accurate currentlimiting, is faster, and can ‘repair’ itself without userintervention.
- The tight currentlimiting evaluated during UL 2367, prevents issuessuch as fires, overheating, bus droop, and supplystress during overloads and startups.
- [[IEC62368]]-1 specifies the safety of electrical and electronicequipment within the field of audio, video, information and communication technology, and business and
- Therefore, if you choose one of TI’s IEC62368-1 certified eFuses, you can usually bypassfurther testing of the device when your final product isevaluated for IEC 62368-1 compliance
- [[IEC 60335]]-1and UL 60730 have a lot in common including ashared definition of a Low-Power Circuit (LPC). For anode to be qualified as a Low-Power Point, themaximum power available at the node should notexceed 15 W
