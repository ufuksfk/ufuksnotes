## Highlights
- **CM:** **Contract Manufacturer**
  • Companies which manufacture parts on behalf of another company. For example, Foxconn is the CM of Apple for making iPhones.
- **JIT:** **Just In Time**
  • A Japanese production strategy that focuses on eliminating waste and keeping inventories lean.
- **CMF:** **Color, Material, and Finish**
  • These are the tactile and aesthetic attributes of a product. Why is this important? Think of iconic products and their impressions on people. anodized aluminum housings on MacBooks, laser etched Apple logos on iPhones, textured instrument panels on the Tesla Model 3, and so on. These design elements are key in enhancing physical user experience.
- **OEM:** **Original Equipment Manufacturer**
- **DFM:** **Design for Manufacturing**
  • Product design and engineering techniques to simplify and increase efficiency of manufacturing processes.
- **DFA:** **Design for Assembly**
  • Product design and engineering techniques to simplify product assembly.
- **FEA:** **Finite Element Analysis**
  • A type of CAE - numerical tools for problem solving of mechanical scenarios (stress, strain, tension, compression, etc.).
- **SPC:** **Statistical Process Control**
  • Using statistical methods to monitor production processes and reduce product defects. The goal of SPC is really to go from a detection to a prevention mindset.
- **FMEA:** **Failure Modes and Effects Analysis**
  • This a risk analysis tool to quantify impact of design and process related failures. Top risks are quantified with a RPN (risk priority number). FMEAS are very common in automotive, consumer electronics, biomedical device, and aerospace.
- **RCA/FA:** **Root Cause Analysis/Failure Analysis**
  • This is the process of identifying the fundamental cause of a problem.
- **CAPA:** **Corrective and Preventive Action Process**
  • This is an approach to identify, analyze, and mitigate issues. Most commonly used in biomedical device development.
- **Poka-Yoke:** **Error-proofing or mistake-proofing techniques**
  • This is a Japanese term which focuses on preventing failure modes. For example - automated torque control guns vs regular screw drivers used to fasten air bag screws to ensure workers do not incorrectly assemble this safety critical component.
- **PRD**: **Product Requirements Document**
  • PRDs are artifacts used to summarize the key requirements and attributes of new products. They help articulate what a product should do to your dev team. These shouldn’t be confused with engineering specs.
