## Highlights
- For the climate concerned, the rise of the AI-reliant internet query is a cause for alarm. Many people have turned to [[ChatGPT]] and other services for simple questions. And even basic Google searches include an AI-derived result.
- Depending on how you crunch the numbers, it’s possible to come up with a wide range for the energy usage and related climate cost of an AI query. ChatGPT provides the [estimate](https://elink54f.time.com/ss/c/u001.XCUcx0YJ_nsHYRCoTi9b2uZqNSZNCGB8_kyqtjAwJ6whBuuudVmIioT_WpCVq1imy1C4cAUxJ0yuKCAnXcsQm7djVKjpWE40z5fF478M0FLTLebZaxUMXOJDAgWVvETmGPbCr-_yMVOcWyS416dKHdz6WRGV9NV-jzPWZcPrSDFWn3OqHvwNdcaxdJgUv4UE47u8SBAxspe2mJdVHcLwgS6M3UWbxs2E5v1b4Qfw74o/4j9/osyBdQzdR2uarza8NaEZrQ/h10/h001.iAhkhooYYxOaxaj7ozqpotHhBdzEZI-a12HdL9DAULI) of up to 0.34 watt-hours per prompt, equivalent to using a household lightbulb for 20 seconds, while one set of researchers [concluded](https://elink54f.time.com/ss/c/u001.KgXVN1lKDCkQ13bdTGLLYpGEJx0YbyXgdU9VsWkdAaxVr_V8zDxHGjTPUORiX-lobFqK0jIpFacRMeupF1GPoZJHy634aosinbRcCcGS-OTr7hlrBXjmd2uVUPEe7JUtpEC96wQHicf733lGOdsSQ89OVz1mxFisbesLyydhpIUL8yYEKo34-5GdvLexlslgnxn1JcCL9UsL44SRMCw1gw/4j9/osyBdQzdR2uarza8NaEZrQ/h11/h001.7I5b31e5XX5rpxVJgAqdFLRS6xZIWDIDiiaAATr6pKA) that some models may use as much as 100 times more for longer prompts.
- On Thursday, [[Google]] released its own data: the average search using Gemini—the company’s ubiquitous AI tool—uses 0.24 watt-hours. That’s equivalent to watching about nine seconds of TV. Such a search emits 0.03 grams of carbon dioxide equivalent. Perhaps more interesting is how Google says Gemini text queries have become cleaner with time. Over the last year, energy consumption per query has decreased around 97% while carbon emissions have decreased by 98% per query, the company said. A separate [report](https://elink54f.time.com/ss/c/u001.PRkXbg-4ycWt7nb-3KBMoIFbu32t9CwiTL4jpRwfulABdQDObBOUdBAnrI-Tq1xl6-W4GbGx9RWFVBA2qZjLjatHD7HHI_r3JBbalMqACusvpQpYXZUnMiWloWLd8TDqAmHUFnhVkliLuzxj-gkun-M7hm0hm_E-AqKRnA7hat3DiDWtD3fqcTgEb_LQjh74NZOsCYIAOM7KJfn6laJAmyGqD5ITcCxSFJsCG5UNQDbH5G-FBe9FivSE8k91EazsYxlqKT_7zop0VS-vNZD9Cg/4j9/osyBdQzdR2uarza8NaEZrQ/h12/h001.xXX7UzhGCvqmEID6gPpEp0ugKpaGLeuH6qAvhN7xk54) from Google released earlier in the summer showed a decoupling of data center energy consumption and the resulting emissions. (It’s worth noting, of course, that simple text queries are less intensive than other functions like image, audio, or visual generation, and these figures don’t include training of models—numbers that aren’t included in Google’s report given the challenges of accurately calculating them). [[Numbers related to ESG]]
- Google’s progress boils down to two levers: cleaner power and more efficient chips and query crunching.
- While Google has done some of that, the most impressive efficiency gains have come through the AI ecosystem rather than the energy system. The company has created its own chips—which it calls TPUs, as opposed to broadly used GPUs. Those chips have become more efficient over time—some 30 times more efficient since 2018, according to Google’s sustainability [report](https://elink54f.time.com/ss/c/u001.PRkXbg-4ycWt7nb-3KBMoIFbu32t9CwiTL4jpRwfulABdQDObBOUdBAnrI-Tq1xl6-W4GbGx9RWFVBA2qZjLjatHD7HHI_r3JBbalMqACusvpQpYXZUnMiWloWLd8TDqAmHUFnhVkliLuzxj-gkun-M7hm0hm_E-AqKRnA7hat3DiDWtD3fqcTgEb_LQjh74NZOsCYIAOM7KJfn6laJAmwD1O0jUumUT6P1PXaS_ML_D7gfhuhccrJKyhvz-W8BWU6BtlVPR-KPAQG7IuBGQhQ/4j9/osyBdQzdR2uarza8NaEZrQ/h13/h001.ltaUJVoJM5K_rdgUWbW8g0tlLV_bMe93eAW1rggYy98).
