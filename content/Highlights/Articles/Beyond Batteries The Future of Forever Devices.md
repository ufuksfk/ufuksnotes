## Highlights
- There is simply no running away from the fact that the tech industry has developed some pretty unsustainable habits over the past few decades - habits like planned obsolescence, short upgrade cycles, proprietary repair mechanisms and deliberately sub-par build qualities have contributed massively to our massive e-waste [problem](https://www.onio.com/article/batteryless-vs-better-recycling-iot-future.html).
- It is [estimated](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/electronic-waste-(e-waste)#:~:text=Key%20facts,collected%20and%20recycled%20(2).) by the WHO that over 62 million tonnes of e-waste was produced in the year 2022! Of this, only a paltry 22.3% was formally recorded to have been recycled. These are truly terrifying numbers!
- 15 billion batteries are produced every year! That’s a crazy number! Here’s another crazy number –  62 million tonnes of e-waste is generated every year, making its way into landfills and our oceans.
- Modern Li-ion batteries use rare minerals that are concentrated in specific parts of the world - Lithium and Cobalt for the most part.
- Cobalt is largely found in one country - the Democratic Republic of Congo - and is mined by child miners who get paid a pittance and end up developing chronic illnesses that severely impact their futures.
- Energy harvesting technology allows devices to capture and utilize energy from various sources:
  • Movement and vibration (kinetic energy)
  • Light (solar energy)
  • Temperature differences (thermal energy)
  • Radio frequency signals (RF energy)
- Moreover, modularity is a major part of this as well - modular designs that allow for repairs and upgrades.
- Customers are willing to pay premium prices for lasting quality. Over the course of their ownership, this would imply lower warranty and support costs - not to mention the brand loyalty that companies stand to build through commitment to ethical practices. Moreover, this also opens the doors to new revenue streams from software and services,.
