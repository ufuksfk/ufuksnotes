## Highlights
- Recently, [our nationally representative survey](https://psychoftech.substack.com/p/unveiling-the-neely-ethics-and-technology) of user experiences with social media platforms has been cited by [several](https://www.thewrap.com/twitter-ceo-toxic-content-enforcement/) [press](https://time.com/6295711/twitters-hate-content-advertisers/) [outlets](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-07-19/twitter-s-surge-in-harmful-content-a-barrier-to-advertiser-return) to suggest that [content on Twitter is more likely to be perceived as “bad for the world”](https://psychoftech.substack.com/p/bad-for-the-world-experiences-on-social-media) and therefore, advertisers should be wary. Twitter has responded to these stories forcefully and below is an excerpt from [a longer tweet](https://twitter.com/lindayacc/status/1681656761101479936) that states that “more than 99% of content users and advertisers see on Twitter is healthy” and “only a small amount of content requires enforcement”.
- For example, in a 4 week period, [23% of Twitter users report seeing content that they believe could increase hate, fear, and/or anger between groups of people and 17% of Twitter users report seeing content they believe could increase the risk of violence](https://psychoftech.substack.com/p/bad-for-the-world-experiences-on-social-media) - numbers which are much higher than other social media platforms.
- [As Facebook has done previously](https://slate.com/technology/2021/11/facebook-good-bad-for-the-world-gftw-bftw.html), it is relatively easy for platforms or stakeholders to ask these questions of their users. Of course, companies may decide to keep these findings private when they do not fit a desired narrative, which means they may not feel the pressure to act on what they find. By publicizing our findings each month, and sharing the data openly with researchers, we hope to improve the quality of the public conversation about this admittedly difficult and messy topic.
