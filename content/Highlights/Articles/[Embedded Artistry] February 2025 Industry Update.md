## Highlights
- [The European Common Charger Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:C_202402997&mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID) is now in effect. Many consumer devices sold in the EU must now provide a [[USB-C]] charging port. The directive lists several examples of portable, battery-powered devices that qualify: "handheld mobile phones, tablets, digital cameras, headphones, headsets, handheld videogame consoles, portable speakers, e-readers, keyboards, mice, portable navigation systems and earbuds." Laptops will be required to meet the directive as well starting 28 April [[2026]].
- Standard charging (≤ 15W) must work with standard [[USB]] power supply options that support "fast charging" (> 15W) and *must* fully support the USB Power Delivery (USB PD) standard.
- March 2024, the U.S. Federal Communications Commission ([[(FCC) U.S. Federal Communications Commission]]) formally adopted [rules for creating a cybersecurity labelling program](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-26A1_Rcd.pdf?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID) (The Internet of Things Cybersecurity Labelling Program and an associated "Cyber Trust Mark"). This was the continuation of the efforts that were defined in the [2023 U.S. National Cybersecurity Strategy executive order](https://embeddedartistry.com/fieldatlas/u-s-national-cyber-security-strategy-2023/?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID).
- In December 2024, [the FCC announced that UL Solutions would be the lead administrator of the labelling program](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-selects-lead-administrator-cybersecurity-label-program?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). The FCC also [announced 10 organizations that can serve as "Cybsersecurity Label Admistrators (CLAs)" under the program](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-announces-10-administrators-iot-labeling-program?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID): CSA America Testing & Certification, CTIA Certification LLC, DEKRA Certification Inc., Intertek Testing Services NA, Inc., ioXt Alliance, Palindrome Technologies, SGS North America Inc., Telecommunications Industry Association, TÜV Rheinland of N.A., and TÜV SÜD America. UL Solutions will also be able to act as a CLA. Each of these CLAs will be authorized by the FCC to certify the use of the Cyber Trust Mark by manufacturers that comply with the program's requirements.
- [The Ferrocene Rust toolchain achieved IEC 62304 Class C qualification](https://ferrous-systems.com/blog/ferrocene-achieves-iec-62304-qualification/?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID), enabling its use for medical device software. [[IEC 62304]]
- [The Linux Kernel is now more than 40 million lines of code](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/linux/linux-kernel-source-expands-beyond-40-million-lines-it-has-doubled-in-size-in-a-decade?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). The source code has doubled since 2015. [[Linux]]
- [GitHub is upgrading their Issues functionality](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-01-13-evolving-github-issues-public-preview/?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID) with sub-issues, issue types, and improved search. [[GitHub]]
- [U-blox announced a phase-out of cellular hardware business](https://www.u-blox.com/en/ir-news/u-blox-announces-strategic-decision-to-increase-focus-on-locate-business-and-phase-out-cellular-as-well-as-an-impairment?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). [[U-blox]]
- [Several git vulnerabilities enabled credential stealing](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/clone2leak-attacks-exploit-git-flaws-to-steal-credentials/?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID).
- [Apple has agreed to pay $95m to settle a lawsuit that alleges Siri routinely recorded private conversations](https://cdn.arstechnica.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Lopez-v-Apple-Unopposed-Motion-for-Preliminary-Approval-of-Class-Action-Settlement-12-31-24.pdf?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). The lawsuit alleges that Siri was recording private conversations that were sold to third parties for targeting ads. The settlement states that these were "unintentional" Siri activations.[[apple]]
- [Logitech announced a partnership with iFixit](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250123655560/en/Logitech-Makes-It-Easier-to-Repair-Your-Electronic-Devices-and-Keep-Them-Longer?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). The company will provide OEM replacement parts for more than 20 Logitech devices on [iFixit's repair hub](https://www.ifixit.com/collaborations/logitech?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). [[Logitech]]
- [Micron started constructing its new high-bandwidth memory packaging facility in Singapore](https://investors.micron.com/news-releases/news-release-details/micron-breaks-ground-new-hbm-advanced-packaging-facility?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). The plant is expected to require $7bn in investment from the company. Operations are expected to begin in 2026, with significant capacity increase in [[2027]]. [[Singapore]]
- [U.S. announced new regulations on global AI chip exports](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2025/01/biden-administration-puts-quotas-on-global-ai-chip-sales/?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). These regulations divide the world into roughly three access tiers. Unrestricted access is allowed for 18 U.S. allies. Bans are maintained for [[China]], [[Russia]], [[Iran]], and [[North Korea]]. Countries in the middle tier can receive up to 50,000 "advanced computing chips," with the possibility for doubling that cap if they sign security agreements with the U.S. The Commerce Department also announced two new verification systems for trusted buyers.
- [U.S. plans to ban new vehicles with software and hardware linked to either China or Russia moves ahead](https://arstechnica.com/cars/2025/01/bidens-ban-on-chinese-and-russian-car-software-moves-ahead/?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID). The final rule will prohibit connected car vehicle software from these nations for model year 2027 onward, and hardware for model year 2030 onward. [[China]] [[Russia]]
- [[Google]] released the [Pebble watch source code](https://github.com/google/pebble?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID) on [[GitHub]]. As in the AVM lawsuit section above, this code delivery is incomplete and not usable on its own. However, it is still a great example of a production code base to explore. [[Pebble]]
- Michael Lynch published useful [guidelines for writing software tutorials](https://refactoringenglish.com/chapters/rules-for-software-tutorials/?mc_cid=b30ce6a679&mc_eid=UNIQID).
