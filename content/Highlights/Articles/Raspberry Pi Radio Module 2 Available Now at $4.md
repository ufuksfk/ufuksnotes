## Highlights
- Today we’re building on that heritage with our first standalone Wi-Fi and Bluetooth radio module. Priced at just $4, [Raspberry Pi Radio Module 2](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/radio-module-2/) packages the same Infineon CYW43439 radio used on [Raspberry Pi Pico W](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/raspberry-pi-pico/) and [Pico 2 W](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/raspberry-pi-pico-2/), and is an ideal choice for [RP2040](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/rp2040/)– and [RP2350](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/rp2350/)-based products that require turnkey wireless connectivity.
- [Raspberry Pi Pico SDK](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/pico-sdk/) and [MicroPython](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/pico/raspberry-pi-pico-python-sdk.pdf) provide a proven, supported software stack, and a straightforward development experience, from prototyping with Pico W or Pico 2 W to production with Radio Module 2.
- You can find technical details of [Radio Module 2](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/radio-module-2/) in its comprehensive [datasheet](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rm2/rm2-datasheet.pdf). It has already seen the light of day in a number of partner products, including SparkFun’s [Thing Plus – RP2350](https://www.sparkfun.com/sparkfun-thing-plus-rp2350.html), and [Pimoroni’s Pico Plus 2 W](https://shop.pimoroni.com/products/pimoroni-pico-plus-2-w), and we’re looking forward to playing spot-the-RM2 in the next wave of connected devices.
